Headlines about young people often rest on a few technical indicators. Each one measures something specific — and something narrower than it sounds. Here is what they mean, based on the official definitions used by Eurostat and the International Labour Organization.

## Youth unemployment rate

The share of young people **in the labour force** who are without work, available to start, and actively looking for a job. The labour force is everyone working or looking for work.

**Common mistake:** reading it as the share of *all* young people who are unemployed. Students who are not looking for work are outside the labour force, so a high rate can apply to a small group. See it mapped in [The Youth Jobs Map](#/data-lab/youth-jobs-map).

## Labour-force participation rate

The share of young people who are either working or looking for work.

**Common mistake:** treating a low rate as a problem in itself. It is often low because many young people are in full-time education.

## NEET rate

The share of young people **not in employment, education or training**, measured against the whole population of that age.

**Common mistake:** assuming every NEET young person is looking for work. The group also includes people caring for family, living with illness or disability, or simply between activities.

## At-risk-of-poverty rate

The share of people whose household income, adjusted for household size, is **below 60% of the national median** after social transfers.

**Common mistake:** reading it as absolute poverty. It measures low income *relative to others in the same country*, so rates are not a direct comparison of living standards between countries. See [The Generation Gap](#/data-lab/generation-gap).

## Housing cost overburden rate

The share of people living in households where total housing costs take up **more than 40% of disposable income**.

**Common mistake:** assuming it covers everyone who struggles with rent. People who have not moved out — or who share with others to keep costs down — may not appear in it. See [The Cost of Being Young](#/data-lab/cost-of-being-young).

## Purchasing power standards (PPS)

An artificial common currency that removes differences in price levels between countries, so that incomes and wages can be compared by what they buy rather than their face value.

**Common mistake:** comparing wages across countries in euros alone. A lower wage can go further where prices are lower. See [The First Paycheck](#/data-lab/first-paycheck).

## One rule for all of them

Always give the age band, the year and the source. “Youth unemployment is high” means little; “the unemployment rate among 15–24-year-olds in the labour force, 2025, ILO modelled estimate” can be checked.
