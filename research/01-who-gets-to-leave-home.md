Across Europe, the age at which young people leave home varies by roughly a decade — from the early twenties in the Nordic countries to over thirty in parts of the south and east. Income explains some of that gap. Policy explains a surprising amount of the rest. This brief compares how different countries help — or fail to help — young people take the first step into a home of their own.

## Key findings

1. Countries with large social, cooperative or non-profit rental sectors see earlier independence, even where youth incomes are lower.
2. Direct cash subsidies for young renters help individuals, but can push up prices where housing supply is fixed.
3. The most effective packages pair supply — homes young people can actually rent — with security of tenure once they are in.

## Method

Comparative review of national and city-level policy, triangulated with published statistics and interviews with young people in each country studied.

**Framework:** Problem → People → Policy → Proof → Lessons
